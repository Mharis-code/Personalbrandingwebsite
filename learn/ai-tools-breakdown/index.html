AI Systems for Businesses — What Are These and Which One to Use?

Four tools. One table. Everything you need to know to pick the right one for where you are.

Haris June 2, 2026 6 min read

Everyone talks about "using AI for your business." Almost nobody explains what that actually means in practice. These four tools represent four completely different approaches to the same problem — making AI actually useful in your daily work. Here is how they compare, when to use each, and the path that makes the most sense for most people starting out.

02

The Key Differences Explained Simply

AI Executive Assistant vs AI Operating System — same tools, different scope

Both are built in Claude Code. Both use context files, skills, and integrations. The difference is what they are built around.

An AI Executive Assistant is built around you: your name, your role, your tasks, your communication style. It helps you do your work faster. It knows your priorities, drafts in your voice, runs your content workflow on a schedule. It serves the person.

An AI Operating System is built around the business: the whole operation, every function, every stakeholder, every revenue stream. It reduces dependency on any single person. It could theoretically run without you. It serves the entity.

For most solopreneurs, the AI Executive Assistant becomes the AI Operating System over time. They start as the same build.

OpenClaw — the middle path that lost its reason to exist

OpenClaw sits between a chat interface and a full code-based agent. More structured than typing into ChatGPT, less powerful than Claude Code. It was a useful stepping stone when Claude Code was harder to use. As Claude Code matured, most power users graduated from it. It is not broken — it has just been made redundant.

Hermes — the layer that runs while your laptop is closed

Hermes is not a replacement for an AI Executive Assistant or AI Operating System — it is the automation layer that works when you are not at your desk. Use cases include: daily AI news briefings posted automatically, social media comment monitoring and responses, morning business summaries, community engagement, server health checks. All running on cron schedules, triggered automatically. You interact with it through your phone via Telegram.

It is the scheduled automation layer of the AI Operating System concept made into a standalone product.

Dispatch vs Hermes for mobile

Dispatch and Hermes both solve the same problem — using your AI from your phone — but in completely different ways.

Dispatch is built into Claude Code. You pair your phone with your desktop, send a task, and Claude works on your computer while you are away. No extra setup, no extra cost. The catch: your computer has to be on and the app has to be running. The moment you close the laptop, Dispatch stops working.

Hermes lives on its own server. It has no dependency on your laptop at all. It runs scheduled tasks at 3am while you are asleep. It responds to you on Telegram when you are at the gym. It monitors your content engagement while you are in a meeting.

The practical rule: if you want to send a quick task to your AI Executive Assistant from your phone and your laptop is on — Dispatch is fine. If you want automations running 24/7 with no dependency on your hardware — that is Hermes.

04

The Recommended Path for Most People

01
Start with the AI Executive Assistant

Build your context files, run the setup interview, create one skill that saves you time daily. Use it for everything — do not go back to a generic chat interface.

02
Let it grow into an OS

As you add more skills and connect more tools, your EA naturally becomes an OS. You do not build an OS from scratch — you grow into one.

03
Add Hermes when you are posting consistently

Once you have a content system running, Hermes handles the scheduled, mobile, and autonomous layer: daily briefings, community engagement, background monitoring.

—
Skip OpenClaw

It served a purpose at a specific moment in AI's development. That moment has passed.
